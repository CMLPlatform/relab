Relab exists to make product disassembly data easier to collect and reuse. These pages explain the research framing, current priorities, and the path from live records to curated dataset releases.

## Why Relab exists

Circular-economy and industrial-ecology research depends on detailed product data — what things are made of, how they come apart, which parts matter, and what evidence backs those claims. That data is scarce, mostly closed, and slow to produce. Producers tend to hold it as proprietary, and the usual alternative — small expert teams sampling products by hand — cannot keep pace with everything entering the market. So methods like life-cycle assessment fall back on generic assumptions, and recovery targets go unverified against real product composition.

Relab takes a bottom-up approach: instead of waiting on producers, it goes downstream to the people who already open products — repairers, refurbishers, dismantlers, recyclers. They meet products at the point of failure, a vantage that captures as-failed composition, wear, and recoverability that as-designed specifications never show. Low-barrier tools turn that routine work into structured, openly-licensed observations, so both the collection *and* the availability of the data are democratized.

The aim is a two-way exchange rather than one-way data entry — a **circular data economy**. Middle- and end-of-life observations feed back into the upstream data infrastructure that research and design rely on, complementing the top-down flow of producer specifications; in return, contributors get value back: composition insight, sustainability metrics, and guidance on the most circular R-strategy for what they are holding.
